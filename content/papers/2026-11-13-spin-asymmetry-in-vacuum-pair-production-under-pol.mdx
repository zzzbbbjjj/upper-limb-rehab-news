## English Summary

We study spin-resolved electron--positron pair production from the quantum vacuum in spatially homogeneous, time-dependent electric fields with linear, elliptical, and circular polarizations. The equal-time Dirac--Heisenberg--Wigner formalism is employed to compute the final momentum distributions in two distinct frequency regimes, for both unchirped and chirped field configurations. In the high-frequency regime, characterized by a reference Keldysh parameter, linearly polarized fields yield identical particle yields for the two spin projections considered, whereas elliptical and circular polarizations generate pronounced spin-resolved population imbalances near the dominant multiphoton resonance. Frequency chirping broadens the resonant spectral structures and extends the momentum range over which a sizable normalized spin difference persists. In the low-frequency regime, the pair-production spectra are governed by quantum tunneling and temporal interference effects. Although the peak spin asymmetry in this regime is substantially weaker than in the high-frequency case, the normalized differential spectra still exhibit sign-changing interference fringes. We find that the spin-asymmetry degree exceeds 90$\%$ for circular polarization in the high-frequency regime, while it saturates at approximately 20$\%$ in the low-frequency regime. Given the purely electric background field, we interpret these results in terms of spin-projected Wigner components and the handed time dependence of rotating electric fields, while clearly distinguishing normalized spectral differences from local or integrated polarization observables.
