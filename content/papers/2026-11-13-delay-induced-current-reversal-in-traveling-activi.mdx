## English Summary

Self-propelled particles that regulate their speed based on a response to a local activity source can move with or against a traveling wave of that source depending on their response time. Silica-titania Janus microswimmers, whose UV-controlled speed relaxes with a delay intrinsic to the titania photoconductivity adaptation, carry two independent memory channels: the familiar directional memory dictated by persistent active Brownian motion, and a distinct memory from the speed adaptation. Combining experiments, Langevin simulations, and a moment expansion of the corresponding Fokker-Planck equation, we show that this delayed response to speed adaptation reshapes transport under traveling UV-illumination waves and can even reverse it relative to the direction set by orientational memory alone. In particular, we observe that delay promotes transport in the direction opposite to the traveling activity waves, where, beyond a critical value, the particle current reverses with respect to the wave propagation direction. Crucially, this behavior requires an asymmetric response to the speed adaptation, with an instantaneous rise but a delayed fall upon exposure to time-varying UV light. Theory further shows that this phenomenology is specific to the response asymmetry rather than to the mere presence of a delay, i.e. reversing the asymmetry reverses the effect of the delay on the particle current relative to the wave. We thus establish propulsion-delay asymmetry as a control parameter for directed transport of responsive active particles in traveling activity landscapes.
