## 中文摘要

密集的主动-被动混合物通常在有效温度图中描述，其中活性掺杂剂促进笼破裂并使被捕获的无定形主体流化。然而，在足够大的持久性下，相同的掺杂剂会导致应力积累和局部空洞形成。在这里，我们研究了整个过程中的雪崩动力学，重点关注动态停止和空隙形成之间的中间状态，其中系统平均保持结构均匀，但通过间歇性集体重排而不是作为简单的活化液体而松弛。通过对嵌入致密非晶固体中的稀释活性掺杂剂进行过阻尼模拟，我们表明雪崩动力学分为不同的空间和时间控制。对于给定的系统尺寸，活性分数设置最大应力释放事件的大小，截止值随着掺杂剂分数 $S_c\sim φ_a^{1/2}$ 的平方根而增长，即随着系统中平均掺杂剂间距的数量而增长...

## English Summary

Dense active--passive mixtures are often described within an effective-temperature picture, in which active dopants promote cage breaking and fluidize an arrested amorphous host. However, at sufficiently large persistence, the same dopants drive stress accumulation and localized void formation. Here we investigate the avalanche dynamics across this progression, focusing on the intermediate regime between dynamic arrest and void formation, where the system remains structurally homogeneous on average but relaxes through intermittent collective rearrangements rather than as a simple activated liquid. Using overdamped simulations of dilute active dopants embedded in a dense amorphous solid, we show that the avalanche dynamics separates into distinct spatial and temporal controls. For a given system size, the active fraction sets the size of the largest stress-release events, with a cutoff that grows as the square root of the fraction of dopants, $S_c\sim φ_a^{1/2}$, i.e., with the number of mean dopant spacings across the system, whereas persistence controls how avalanches unfold in time at fixed released stress. A decomposition of the stress into active and passive contributions, together with measurements of avalanche participation and morphology, shows that the active minority provides a localized and persistent driving channel while the surrounding amorphous packing carries the collective stress release. While the active minority controls the cutoff of the avalanche size statistics and the timing of the stress releases, the long-range elastic response and the building correlations in the passive host sets the size exponent, $τ_S\simeq1.1$, that is shared with externally sheared and uniformly active amorphous solids.
