## English Summary

The False Nearest Neighbours (FNN) algorithm is one of the most widely used methods for choosing the embedding dimension, i.e. the number $k$ of delay coordinates, in the time-delay reconstruction of a dynamical system from an observed scalar time series. We prove rigorous results on the behaviour of this algorithm for long time series. We consider a smooth dynamical system on a compact manifold $M$ and an invariant probability measure $μ$. For a typical measurement function and $μ$-almost every trajectory, we show that the proportion of false nearest neighbours tends to zero (provided that the tolerance threshold is large enough) once $k &gt;\dim M$. On the other hand, for systems with super-polynomial decay of correlations, we prove that this proportion tends to one whenever $k$ is smaller than the Hausdorff dimension of $μ$. Consequently, in this case, the FNN algorithm (run for long enough time series) returns an embedding dimension $k$ satisfying $\dim_H μ\leq k \leq \dim M + 1$. This is below the threshold $2\dim M + 1$ required by the Takens embedding theorem, so the classical Takens embedding theory does not explain why reconstructions and predictions with $k$ chosen by the FNN algorithm work in practice. The explanation for this case is provided by a recent probabilistic theory of time-delay embeddings. We apply our results to the Hénon and Lorenz attractors, showing that the FNN algorithm typically returns $k=2$ for the Hénon map (in the chaotic case) and $k \leq 3$ for the Lorenz system.
