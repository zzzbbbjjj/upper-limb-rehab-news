## English Summary

We present a numerical study of contact-force organization in dense assemblies of active, deformable cells. Using the Fourier Contour Cell model, which enables efficient simulation of large cell deformations, we investigate how cell deformability and active motility shape spatial organization of intercellular forces. We find that, in the soft liquid regime at intermediate self-propulsion strength, the contact-force distribution becomes bimodal, reflecting the emergence of a distinct population of weak interactions. Persistent-homology analysis further reveals a characteristic force-network structure in which weak tip-tip interactions are enclosed by loops of strong side-side interactions. Simulations with elongated but non-deformable cells demonstrate that both the bimodal force distribution and the associated network motif require cell deformability rather than elongated cell shape per se. Furthermore, introducing a curvature constraint shows that local cell curvature regulates the strength of weak tip-tip interactions. Together, these results demonstrate that cell deformability and local geometric constraints can organize force transmission across dense active cellular assemblies.
