## English Summary

Practical modeling of disordered single-layer confluent tissues has relied heavily on the two-dimensional standard vertex model (SVM), which simplifies cell elasticity using an energy functional determined by quadratic dependence of cell area and perimeter values. Realistically, even a coarse-grained cell interior consists of effective continuum materials representing cytoplasm and peripheral cortex, with elastic energy depending on stress and strain fields. It is shown that this energy is analytically tractable for polygonal cell shapes, that its area dependence can be eliminated, that it correlates well with cell perimeter length, and that the perimeter dependence of the correlation is not quadratic. This results in a revised vertex model derived from continuum elasticity whose parameters are predicted from tissue material properties and whose resultant forces are far less strain-dependent than in the SVM.
