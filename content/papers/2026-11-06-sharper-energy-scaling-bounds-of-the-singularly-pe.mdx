## English Summary

In this article, we discuss the scaling behaviour of the singularly perturbed Tartar square, revisiting the results by [Rüland-Tribuzio '22]. Therein, it was proven that both lower and upper bounds satisfy a behaviour of the same type, up to a small mismatch of logarithmic powers. We show by an improvement of the ansatz free lower bound that the logarithmic powers indeed match and that the bounds are of order $\exp( - C \log(\varepsilon^{-1})^{1/2} )$ for suitable constants $C = C_{\rm low}$ and $C = C_{\rm upp}$, where $\varepsilon$ denotes the singular perturbation parameter. More specifically, the bootstrap argument in the Fourier space is refined by resorting to Fourier multipliers enjoying $L^\infty$-bounds which are constructed via de la Vallée Poussin kernels. Regarding upper bounds, we perform iterated branching constructions sharpening the upper bound of [Winter '97] which resorted to an iteration of laminates as a competitor. Eventually, the mismatch of upper and lower bound is quantified, and for quadratic energies we obtain the bounds $C_{\rm low} \leq 7.0339$ and $C_{\rm upp} \geq 2 \log(2) - ν$ for any $ν&gt;0$.
