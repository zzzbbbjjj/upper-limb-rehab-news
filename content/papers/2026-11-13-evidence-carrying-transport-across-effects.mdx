## English Summary

A program logic proved sound against one effectful semantics need not remain sound when the effect is re-implemented, yet the semantic relationship between reasoning before and after a change of effect is usually left implicit and re-established by hand. This paper provides a uniform, proof-relevant account of it. Working in realizability, where a notion of computation determines a model whose entailments carry explicit program evidence, we introduce simulations between effectful realizability cores, generalizing Longley's applicative morphisms to computation over an arbitrary strong monad. A single simulation transports the source logic along a ladder: it induces a morphism of the associated evidenced frames and, functorially, of the induced triposes. These passages are $2$-fully-faithful, so the three levels agree and a change of effect carries the entire higher-order program logic together with the evidence witnessing each derivation. A computationally dense simulation moreover computes a modality on the source logic, a nucleus through which the transport factors and which marks exactly the reasoning stable under the change, read off from the translation rather than postulated. This recovers known examples such as the double-negation modality of classical realizability, and examples such as sheafification arise from a modal refinement of the construction. Overall, the framework makes a change of effect a first-class object, one that can be composed, factored through its modality, and shown in cases not to exist, opening a path to transporting verified reasoning across effects.
