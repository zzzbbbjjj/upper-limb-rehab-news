## English Summary

This paper investigates the robustness of pinching-antenna systems (PASSs) to three practical deployment imperfections: pinching-antenna (PA) positioning errors, imperfect channel state information (CSI), and line-of-sight (LoS) blockages. We develop analytical frameworks to quantify the performance degradation caused by each imperfection. For PA positioning errors, we employ a Brownian meander model to characterize the distribution and mean of the resulting signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) loss. The results show that positioning errors can diminish the gains from long-term position selection, with the loss becoming more pronounced under stronger shadow fading. For imperfect CSI, we characterize the distribution and moments of the true scattered channel power at the position selected using noisy channel estimates. The resulting selection penalty is governed by the ratio of noise variance to the number of channel measurements, while the upper tail of the selected channel power retains the same behavior as under perfect CSI. Finally, we develop a spatially consistent blockage model and derive the distribution of the optimum long-term SNR for arbitrary blockage configurations, together with a numerically tractable expression for a single blockage. Simulation results validate the analyses and highlight a fundamental property of PASSs: although positioning errors and imperfect CSI can reduce the gains from position selection, good performance can still be achieved provided that an LoS link is available. In particular, even when a large proportion of the waveguide is blocked, PA repositioning can establish LoS links and enable performance close to that of a fully unblocked system for the considered configurations.
