## English Summary

Bent liquid-crystalline dimers provide a model system for studying the spontaneous emergence of chirality from achiral molecules. Depending on terminal-chain length, these materials exhibit a competition between heliconical, synclinic, and anticlinic packing tendencies in tilted smectic phases. In short homologues, the tendency to form twist-bend phases dominates, leading to two distinct helical phases: the SmCTB-SH phase, with a helical periodicity of four molecular lengths, and at lower temperatures, a double helical structure - the SmCTB-DH phase, with progressive rotation of the four-layer unit. With increasing terminal chain length, this twist-bend tendency is gradually suppressed, favoring phases with simple synclinic and anticlinic tilt arrangement in successive layers. Remarkably, in the regime where these tendencies become comparable, a new modulated smectic phase, SmCTB-mod develops; a double helical structure in which the internal four-layer unit undergoes a periodic modulation. This additional level of structural organization provides a means of accommodating competing heliconical and synclinic/anticlinic tendencies and produces a characteristic splitting of the resonant x-ray diffraction peaks into four signals symmetrically located around a signal of four-layer periodicity. These results reveal how bent dimers resolve local-packing-mode frustration through complex spontaneous hierarchical structural organization.
