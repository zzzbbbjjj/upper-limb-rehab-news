## English Summary

The so-called ``100% rule'' is a somewhat classical and yet somehow obscure rule that gives some local sensitivity-analysis information based on the very limited local sensitivity-analysis information that is often made available by linear-programming software, derived from an optimal basis that is hidden from the user. Introduced in a classic undergraduate textbook in 1977, half a century ago, it was only picked up on by researchers in a very limited way, and while it appears in some undergraduate textbooks, it tends not to appear in most modern graduate-level textbooks. Our contributions are to celebrate and revive interest in the 100% rule and to extend it in a meaningful way. We introduce a cone rule that certifies primal feasibility for more right-hand sides than the 100% rule does, using the same information, and that can be applied in linear time. We then study when this cone rule is tight, that is, when it captures the entire cone of right-hand sides for which the basis remains feasible. We characterize tightness in every dimension for right-hand sides interior to that cone, make the characterization explicit for m=2 and m=3, and settle the question for tridiagonal bases in every dimension: tightness is impossible once m&gt;=4, and for m=3 we give an explicit criterion in the entries of the basis. We give further results for symmetric, integer and unimodular bases. Finally, when tightness fails, we measure the fraction of the feasibility cone captured by the rule using normalized solid angles. For the autoregressive family at m=3, we obtain a closed-form candidate for the infimum of this fraction over all right-hand sides, whose optimality is supported numerically. We also obtain a closed-form expression for the best case over most of the correlation range, subject to two numerically verified properties, and estimate the best-case value numerically more generally.
