## English Summary

Conventional two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) inference need not be uniformly adequate even when all treatment weights are positive. Under a local treatment-effect drift, the statistic for the equally weighted treated-cell effect has worst-case mean shift $(c/q)Γ$, where $Γ=\sqrt{N_1}\|w-u\|_2$ is design-only and $q$ is the cluster-score noise scale. Projection matches the exposure to cohort, event-time, additive, and saturated group-time pilots; when residualized treatment is constant within treated group-time cells, $Γ_{\mathrm{gt}}=Γ$. A direct score estimator is feasible with fixed panel length as clusters grow. Because a debiased quadratic pilot is nonregular at zero, we use two regular one-sided procedures: a reverse-triangle multiplier lower bound to detect unsafe envelopes and covariance-aware noncentral-$χ^2$ inversion to certify adequacy. At $5\%$ nominal size with a five-point tolerance, the shift threshold is $0.652$. In the Callaway--Sant'Anna minimum-wage panel, where every treated-cell weight is positive, the saturated $95\%$ lower shift bound is $0.793$, so conventional inference is not uniformly certified. A complementary Brazilian property-tax reanalysis with $2{,}840$ municipalities withholds certification in all four classes; its smallest lower bound is $1.040$. Under the maintained many-cluster approximation, castle-doctrine homicide passes for additive cohort-plus-event-time heterogeneity with a $95\%$ upper bound of $0.236$; its saturated covariance lacks one required rank. Divorce is inconclusive. These envelopes are not predictions of realized rejection. The diagnostic separates point correction, uniform exposure, and statistically warranted certification.
