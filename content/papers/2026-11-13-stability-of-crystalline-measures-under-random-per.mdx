## English Summary

In this paper, we study random perturbations and the diffraction properties of translation-bounded crystalline measures. Under a non-vanishing assumption on the known perturbation factor, we prove that, almost surely, any realization of the perturbed measure preserves sufficient structural information to allow the full recovery of the original measure. Previous works have only considered crystalline measures with uniformly discrete supports and equal-mass atoms. Our general setting includes many important crystalline measures that were excluded until now. To treat this broader setting, we adopt a different framework. In this work, we model the perturbation as a random measure rather than as a random set. In particular, this leads us to consider not only perturbations of the support, but also perturbations of the atoms' masses. On the other hand, using elementary techniques of Fourier analysis, we prove that any translation-bounded crystalline measure has a diffraction measure, and we explicitly compute its diffraction.
