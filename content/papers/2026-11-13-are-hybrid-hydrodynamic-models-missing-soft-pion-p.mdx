## English Summary

In this work, we perform a systematic Bayesian analysis of the full parameter space of a state-of-the-art hybrid hydrodynamical model and show that no combination of parameters provides a satisfactory description of the low-$p_T$ region of the pion $(π^{\pm})$ transverse-momentum spectra. This suggests that an additional mechanism enhancing pion production at low $p_T$ may be needed to properly describe the ALICE data. We first reexamine and confirm the existence of an excess of low $p_T$ pions relative to model predictions, showing that no comparable excess is observed in the spectra of kaons $(K^\pm)$, protons $(p)$, or antiprotons $(\bar{p})$. We further demonstrate that this excess persists under a rigorous Bayesian analysis of simulations that include, e.g., a hadronic afterburner, a feature omitted in previous similar studies. Despite the observed excess, the scaled pion spectra retain their universal behavior, exhibiting little dependence on collision centrality. In fact, pions exhibit the most pronounced universality among the particle species considered. We then estimate the effects of two candidate mechanisms for generating the low-$p_T$ pion enhancement by introducing a one-parameter extension of the model into the Bayesian analysis. We find that the data favor enhancement mechanisms whose contributions decrease with increasing $p_T$, becoming negligible at high-$p_T$. Our results motivate further investigations of non-equilibrium effects in hybrid hydrodynamic simulations.
