## English Summary

The Anderson effect, arising from destructive quantum interference of electron wave packets in disordered potentials, is usually observed at cryogenic temperatures and typically arises from extrinsic defects in conventional materials such as phosphorus dopants in silicon or oxygen vacancies in transition metal oxides. Here we demonstrate that a multication lattice of transition metals in a high-entropy oxide (HEO) natively establishes an interacting Anderson insulator that extends up to $500\,\mathrm{K}$. By controlling the population of transition metal cations in $\mathrm{La}_{0.7}\mathrm{Sr}_{0.3}\mathrm{Mn}_{1-x}(\mathrm{Cr},\mathrm{Fe},\mathrm{Co},\mathrm{Ni})_{x/4}\mathrm{O}_3$ (LS5B), we tune the system from the prototypical correlated metal $\mathrm{La}_{0.7}\mathrm{Sr}_{0.3}\mathrm{Mn}\mathrm{O}_3$ at $x=0.00$ to a localized insulator at $x=0.80$, where the disorder length contracts to near the unit cell (3.85 $\mathring{\mathrm{A}}$) and drives the electron mean free path to the Ioffe-Regel limit. Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) reveals the systematic localization of itinerant $d$-quasiparticle states at the Fermi level, consistent with the emergence of a soft Coulomb gap, while the underlying band structure and symmetry are preserved. Transport reveals Efros-Shklovskii variable-range hopping governed by a colossal soft gap with characteristic temperature $T_{\mathrm{ES}}&gt;50{,}000\,\mathrm{K}$ and screened Coulomb interaction strengths as high as 1.6 eV. Together, multiple lines of evidence indicate that interacting Anderson behavior is a native characteristic that survives to temperatures as high as $500\,\mathrm{K}$ in high-entropy materials.
