## English Summary

Complementary metal-oxide semiconductor (CMOS) image sensors offer distinct advantages as imaging detectors for space-based X-ray observatories, being low-power, radiation-resistant, and operable at room temperature. Previous characterizations of the low-cost, commercially-available IMX290LLR-C back-illuminated CMOS sensor from Sony Semiconductor Solutions have found Fano-limited X-ray spectral resolutions while operated at room temperature and a radiation tolerance compatible with use in low Earth orbit. Here, we report the quantum efficiency of the IMX290LLR-C as measured across the soft X-ray band (200 - 1000 eV) using Beamline 6.3.2 of the Advanced Light Source at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. We find a quantum efficiency of roughly 10 -- 40\% over this band, and fit these measurements to a quantum efficiency model constrained by these measurements and microscopy of the sensor layers. These measurements and the derived model informs this sensor's application within low-cost X-ray observatories, as well as motivates the development of commercial X-ray CMOS sensors without microlenses and deeper Si depletion layers.
