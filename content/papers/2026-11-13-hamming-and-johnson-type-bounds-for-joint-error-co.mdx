## English Summary

We derive upper bounds on the size of codes for joint error correction and detection over classical $q$-ary Hamming spaces. A $(c,c')$ joint error correcting and detecting code corrects every error of weight at most $c$ and detects every error whose weight lies between $c+1$ and $c+c'$. We establish equivalent conditions for this decoding requirement using Hamming balls and Hamming rings, and derive a joint bound that reduces to the Hamming bound when $c'=0$. We introduce joint perfect codes and show that binary repetition codes attain the joint bound whenever $n=2c+c'+1$. For codes satisfying $d_{\min}=2c+c'+1$, we investigate an alternative joint bound based on ring multiplicity and constant-weight code bounds. A further argument using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and pairwise ring intersections yields a refined joint bound that is always at least as strong as the original joint bound. We then incorporate an additional outer Hamming shell to obtain a Johnson-type extension. This extension provides a strict improvement for binary codes with $c'=1$, $d_{\min}=2c+2$, $c\geq2$, and $d_{\min}+2\leq n\leq3d_{\min}/2-1$. These results connect sphere-packing bounds with the geometry of joint error correction and detection.
