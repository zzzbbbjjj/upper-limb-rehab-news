## English Summary

Sparse vector coding (SVC) and its variants convey information through sparse vectors (SVs), or sparse support patterns and detecting information by solving sparse recovery problems, making them attractive for short-packet ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC). However, as the required information rate increases, conventional SVC schemes or the enhanced SVC (ESVC) schemes require a rapidly growing number of SVs, resulting in significant increase of complexity and receiver processing latency. On the other hand, when communicating in doubly selective channels, orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) suffers from the Doppler spread induced inter-carrier interference, while affine frequency division multiplexing (AFDM) experiences the same peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR) problem as OFDM, although it allows diversity gain from channel's double selectivity. To address these issues, this paper proposes a constant-weight sparse vector coded augmented AFDM (CW-SVC/A$^2$FDM ) scheme, along with a corresponding joint support and symbol detection algorithm. In this scheme, SVs are represented by constant-weight codewords that feature a fixed Hamming weight and a large Hamming distance, resulting in relatively small SV sets. Simulation results compare various SVC/ESVC schemes and diverse coding schemes employed by the CW-SVC/A$^2$FDM, including Hamming, BCH, RS, and polar codes, for QAM symbol protection. The results demonstrate that the proposed scheme provides a distinct reliability advantage over conventional SVC/ESVC schemes, achieving a gain of up to 14 dB. Furthermore, the study indicates that the CWC's weight and minimum Hamming distance must be carefully selected to balance detection reliability, QAM modulation order, and sparse recovery feasibility, thereby maximizing the overall system's performance.
