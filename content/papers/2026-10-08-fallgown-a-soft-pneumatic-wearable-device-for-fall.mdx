## English Summary

Concerns about falling and potential resulting injuries prevent many older adults from participating in forms of health-improving exercise such as walking, dance, and tai chi. Existing passive devices like walkers require use of the arms for support, and active devices like exoskeletons and robotic frames are often rigid, bulky, and stigmatized, which limits their use. The FallGown is a soft wearable support structure consisting of pressurized pneumatic pouches within a fabric shell; it can support the full weight of a user's body and aims to prevent falls. It is portable (6.9 kg and 0.051 m3 when stored), minimally burdensome (7.24-20.8 N drag force depending on floor type and whether an optional rolling base frame is used), and convenient (less than 2.5 minutes on average to don and less than 0.5 minutes on average to doff). The optional rolling base lowers friction with the floor, but reduces portability and skirt deformation (which can be useful to fit through doors and other tight spaces). Fall prevention testing with a single user revealed a tradeoff between device size and stability against tipping; a skirt with 12 pneumatic pouches and a floor diameter of 1.4 meters with a base frame prevented all falls except in one case with a mechanical failure. On-board pressure sensors can sense the user's weight distribution, providing an opportunity for monitored exercise and adaptive support.
