## English Summary

In Reference [Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 144003 (2026)], the authors claim the 'kinematic' and 'rheological' equivalence between simple shear and planar extension. They state that their result holds "for any materially objective constitutive equation." The proposed relation is exact for single-mode constitutive models whose stress is coaxial with a single tensor obeying an evolution equation that depends linearly on the velocity gradient. However, it fails for standard kinetic theories of dilute solutions, e.g. FENE dumbbells, and for the integral tube or K-BKZ models of entangled solutions and melts. Their result is a consequence of the employed closures, not of an underlying equivalence between the flows. Its accuracy for real fluids cannot be inferred from such closed models and instead requires independent validation against measured planar extensional data.
