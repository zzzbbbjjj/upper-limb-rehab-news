## English Summary

Beam selection at large passive reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) requires either full channel knowledge or beam training, both of which entail unwanted overhead. On the other hand, learned models that predict the optimal codeword from channels sampled at a few active elements avoid both, at the cost of energy-demanding multiply-and-accumulate operations. In this paper, we propose spiking neural network (SNN)-based beam selection for a semi-active RIS assisting a mmWave multiple-input single-output communication system. Direct and sigmoid rate coding are compared against a multi- layer perceptron (MLP) of identical architecture. Energy analysis further reveals that the input layer dominates SNN energy consumption.
