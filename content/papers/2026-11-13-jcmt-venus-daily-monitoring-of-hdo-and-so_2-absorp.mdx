## English Summary

We present three multi-week, disc-integrated James Clerk Maxwell Telescope (JCMT) campaigns monitoring HDO and SO$_2$ absorption in Venus' upper mesosphere at altitudes of approximately 80--110 km. Obtained in February 2022, July 2023, and August--September 2023, these observations provide dense temporal sampling and complementary observing geometries, enabling the disentanglement of short-term, long-term, and local solar time (LST) variability, which has been challenging in previous studies. We have four main findings. First, SO$_2$ in the upper mesosphere, being largely absent in February 2022 but present in both 2023 campaigns, may follow the same long-term trend observed at cloud tops. Second, long-term variability in H$_2$O is identified here for the first time and is interpreted as an anticorrelated response to the long-term change in SO$_2$, likely through H$_2$SO$_4$ formation. Third, H$_2$O is broadly stable within each campaign, although occasional short-term variability is present in the form of a depletion event. Finally, short-term variability is likely present for SO$_2$, but it does not dominate the overall behaviour. In the absence of strong long-term differences, the daily SO$_2$ abundance evolution is consistent with an asymmetric LST profile peaking near midnight, which is well reproduced by an independent model. These results are enabled by a dedicated reduction and retrieval pipeline that mitigates the strong standing-wave baseline structure in the spectra and quantifies systematic uncertainties, providing a methodological framework for future studies facing similar challenges.
