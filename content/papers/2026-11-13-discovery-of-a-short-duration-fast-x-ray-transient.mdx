## English Summary

Extragalactic fast X-ray transients (EFXTs) are an emerging population of high-energy transients with diverse physical origins. Only a few observed EFXTs are associated with compact object mergers, and the known events generally display X-ray emission lasting much longer than their associated short gamma-ray bursts (GRBs). Here we report EP260411a, an $\sim8$-s EFXT discovered in archival Einstein Probe Wide-field X-ray Telescope (EP-WXT) observations through a time-targeted search for X-ray counterparts of short GRBs. This fast X-ray transient is temporally coincident with short GRB 260411A, with both the pulse width and peak time showing clear anticorrelations with the characteristic photon energy across the gamma-ray and soft X-ray bands. Joint EP-WXT and Fermi-GBM spectral fitting further shows that the broadband emission can be described by a cutoff power-law model with an additional low-energy spectral break, suggesting a common origin for the single-pulse X-ray and gamma-ray emission. The resulting properties place the event within the Type I GRB region in the $E_{\rm p,z}$-$E_{\rm iso}$ plane, supporting its classification as a compact object merger event. Unlike previously known merger-driven Type I EFXTs with long-lasting X-ray emission, EP260411a reveals that the Type I EFXT population can also extend to the short timescale of a few seconds. Its combination of short duration and hard spectrum occupies a sparsely explored region of the EFXT parameter space, providing a new observational link between short-duration EFXTs and compact object mergers.
