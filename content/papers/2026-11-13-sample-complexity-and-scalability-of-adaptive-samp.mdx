## English Summary

Stochastic trust-region methods are usually analyzed in expectation or with high probability: the guarantee is about the law of the algorithm, not about the run in hand. This paper analyzes the adaptive sampling stochastic trust-region method ASTRO-DF (which is the reuse variant ASTRA-R in Part I) in random subspaces along almost every sample path. The route has one probabilistic ingredient, the accuracy of the common-random-number estimates at the adaptive sample size, uniformly over the trust region; Part~I established it by chaining, here it is obtained at the stopping time by optional stopping and converted, by the first Borel--Cantelli lemma, into an accuracy that holds from a random index on, after which the deterministic trust-region argument applies. We prove almost sure convergence and almost sure iteration and sample complexity of orders $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(dε^{-2})$ and $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^2ε^{-4})$, with a subspace dimension growing logarithmically; we show that at a fixed subspace dimension the eventual-accuracy argument yields $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(ε^{-2-\mathcal{O}(1/p)})$ iterations and $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(ε^{-4-\mathcal{O}(1/p)})$ samples; we show that the random index after which the rates hold cannot be too large and has an explicit, rapidly decaying tail. Compared with the supermartingale route, the pathwise route needs no lower bound on the per-iteration probability of a good iteration, no coupling between the trust-region expansion factor and the sketch, and no moment condition on the summed squared radii.
