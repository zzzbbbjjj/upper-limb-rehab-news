## English Summary

Slope change detection seeks to identify changes in the trends of time-ordered signals. We study the problem of fitting a multidimensional time series by continuous piecewise-linear functions with a shared continuous breakpoint and unrestricted slopes. We consider both the dense setting, in which every dimension may change slope, and the sparse setting, in which at most $k$ dimensions may change slope. Although the resulting optimization problems are nonconvex mixed integer nonlinear programs and may have irrational optimal solutions even for rational data, we prove that all six variants, corresponding to the $\ell_1$, $\ell_2$, and $\ell_\infty$ losses in both settings, are exactly solvable in polynomial time. For least-squares loss, we obtain efficient algorithms requiring $O(JT)$ operations in the dense setting and $O(J^2T\log J)$ operations in the sparse setting, where $T$ is the number of time points and $J$ is the number of dimensions. We implement these least-squares algorithms and evaluate them on simulated data and three real-world time series, namely, surface temperatures (ERA5), hand-tracking data from a mixed-reality headset (HoloAssist), and turbofan engine sensor data (NASA C-MAPSS). The dense algorithm solves instances with up to $J=10^5$ and $T=100$, that is, $10^7$ observations, in less than $0.1$ seconds on a laptop, while the sparse algorithm solves instances with about $10^3$ dimensions and up to $T=240$ in under a second. Compared with a mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) formulation solved by Gurobi, our algorithms are several orders of magnitude faster (approximately $8.9 \times 10^3$ to $3.4 \times 10^6$ times) on instances where Gurobi terminates within its default optimality tolerance of $0.01\%$ optimality gap. On larger instances, Gurobi frequently reaches a 10,000-second time limit with substantial optimality gaps.
