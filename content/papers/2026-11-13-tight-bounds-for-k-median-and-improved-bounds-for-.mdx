## English Summary

The popular $k$-means++ algorithm, or $D^\ell$-sampling, is a fundamental primitive in both the theory and practice of clustering. In the bi-criteria setting, the algorithm samples $k+Δ$ centers ($Δ\ge0$), and its cost is compared with the optimum using $k$ centers. Despite extensive study over the past two decades, its exact approximation ratio and tight bi-criteria guarantees remain unknown.   We give a unified analysis for every distance exponent $\ell&gt;0$, every $k\ge1$, and every $Δ\ge0$. For metric $k$-median, our bounds are tight for all $k$ and $Δ$. Without extra centers, the exact approximation ratio is $2(1+H_{k-1})$ (also in Euclidean space), improving the previous upper bound by a factor of $2$ ($H_j$ denotes the $j$th harmonic number). This tight analysis reveals that even one extra center improves the approximation ratio by an additive $Θ(\log\log k)$, whereas previous bounds show no improvement.   For every $\ell&gt;0$, our analysis gives upper bounds with the same dependence on $k$ and $Δ$ as our tight bound for metric $k$-median, with leading constants depending only on $\ell$ and whether the metric is Euclidean. Beyond the improved dependence on $Δ$, our leading constants are smaller than those in previous analyses. In particular, for the widely used Euclidean $k$-means objective, our upper bound without extra centers is $(1+\varphi)(1+H_{k-1})$, where $\varphi\approx1.618$ is the golden ratio, improving upon the previous constant of $5$. With oversampling, a constant fraction of extra centers is known to provide a constant-factor approximation. For example, with $10\%$ extra centers, our guarantee is $5.7$, compared with the best previous guarantee of $25.9$.
